The benchmark

Three actions in a reflective world

A reflection can look like a corridor or duplicate the green goal beacon. The agent must enter the actual goal cell. Each planning round may contain a sequence of actions. A collision does not terminate a valid sequence.

↶leftRotate one heading
↑forwardAttempt one adjacent cell
↷rightRotate one heading

Square cells use 90° turns and hexagonal cells use 60°. The visual protocol supplies first-person images after each attempted action plus execution feedback. The text protocol supplies ordered action outcomes only during play. Agents receive no review map.

Main findings

An exit tells only part of the story

Text cleared more goals under the planning cap. Vision cleared more goals within a small collision budget. The route, the number of wall hits, and coverage show how those outcomes were achieved.

How agents spend actions and hit walls

01

A planning call can buy a long sequence of probes.

Gemini 3.8 Flash cleared Easy Square MIRROR-042 in 9 text rounds with 268 wall hits, compared with 116 visual rounds and 40 hits. Sol cleared all eight maps in both arms. On Easy Hex MIRROR-042, vision took 315 actions with zero wall hits. Text took 8,846 actions with 3,823 hits. Across its eight maps, Sol’s median fell from 889.5 hits in text to 12 in vision.

02

When an agent repeats a hit matters.

On the first attempts at the cell and heading pairs they chose to probe, Sol hit walls on 9.1% of visual attempts and 56.7% of text attempts. Gemini showed 28.8% versus 54.1%. These are policy-selected poses.

When a hit had another action in the same submitted batch, the immediate next action hit the same face in 31.6% of text events and 21.8% of visual events. After a round-ending hit and fresh feedback, the next round’s first forward attempt hit the same face in 1.2% and 4.5%, respectively. DeepSeek accounted for 94 of 96 visual repeats.

On revisits to a previously hit pose after 21 or more rounds, 39.9% of text and 3.9% of visual plans started by moving into the wall. The policies revisit different poses at different times, so this motivates a controlled memory test.

03

Coverage reveals stalled or repetitive exploration.

Sol hit only 2 walls on Hard Hex MIRROR-043 and saw no new cell in its final 581 rounds. DeepSeek took 111,088 actions and 33,607 physical wall hits on Medium Hex MIRROR-043 and still visited only 69 of 127 cells.

04

Repeated images are a candidate explanation.

DeepSeek’s visual runs recorded roughly 49,000 delivered image entries, about 97% duplicates, and 744 rounds with image trimming. Repeated movement produces redundant views. Whether redundant views also impair planning calls for a controlled image-history comparison.

A blind policy can solve the original mazes

A fixed wall follower cleared 8/8 original loop-free mazes using only success or wall feedback. All canonical starts face their only opening. Across all initial headings it finished within budget on 36/40 trials. Random turns and forward probes with no feedback cleared 23/4,096 within 800 primitive actions.

Adding 12 passages to each map reduced fixed wall-following success within budget to 55.2%. Depth-first search with relative position memory still reached 98.0%. An ideal local wall sensor yields a separate collision-free reference of 31–153 passage decisions on the original maps.

In 16 mirror-versus-matte image pairs, some blocked directions showed the goal’s reflection only in the mirror image. The model pilot yielded no complete paired verdicts. The rendering effect is visible, while the rate of model misclassification remains open.

The hexagonal gap depends on the protocol

In Easy vision, square maps cleared 6/8 and hexagonal maps 2/8. In Easy text, both shapes reached 7/8. Sol cleared both Hard square maps and neither Hard hexagonal map even though they have 225 and 217 cells, respectively. Heading changes, local connectivity, rendering, and the two map seeds per shape all matter.

In the three failed Sol API runs, 868 of 970 wall hits came from the final planning batch. The earlier traces often rotated or stopped discovering new cells. Prompting, conversation management, and reasoning-state retention vary with the system. The Gemini API control uses a different model from the native Flash profile.

How interrupted cases are counted

Four Flash Hard trials remain not evaluated because provider quota notices interrupted their usable trajectories. Two Easy Gemini 4 Argon API trials remain paused. These cases, the four policy skips, and one withdrawal are outside measured-outcome denominators.
